# Research Brief: Academic / Subject Exams for University Admission (September 2026)

## SAT (Digital SAT)
- **Full name + body**: Scholastic Assessment Test (Digital SAT), administered by College Board (US)
- **Purpose**: Undergraduate admission, primarily US universities; widely used by US institutions for international applicants; some Canadian, and a handful of Asian/European universities also accept it
- **Format**: Computer-adaptive (multistage), taken in the Bluebook app at test centers. Reading & Writing: 2 modules, 64 min, 54 questions; Math: 2 modules, 70 min, 44 questions (calculator/Desmos throughout). Total ~2h14m. Score 400–1600 (200–800 per section)
- **Cost**: $68 US; $111 international ($68 + $43 flat international fee) per College Board fees page (valid through at least 2025–26; fees reviewed annually)
- **Frequency**: 7 US dates/year (Mar, May, Jun, Aug, Oct, Nov, Dec); 6 international dates (no Nov)
- **Score validity**: No official expiry; scores reportable indefinitely, though some colleges prefer scores from the last ~5 years
- **Popularity**: ~1.9–2.0M students per graduating class (class of 2024: 1.9M+; ~1.97M cited for 2024 — College Board Total Group report)
- **Prep-hub content scope**: RW grammar/reading question types, algebra/advanced math/problem solving/geometric-trig; module-adaptive full-length Bluebook-style mocks; question bank by difficulty tier; score calculators and percentile tables; Old (paper) vs Digital concordance for legacy content; college score Ranges/SuperScore policy pages
- **Relevance (international)**: **5** for US undergrad; demand rising as US test-optional policies reverse (MIT, Harvard, Yale, Stanford, Caltech, etc. reinstated testing 2024–26)

## ACT
- **Full name + body**: ACT (American College Testing), administered by ACT, Inc. (US)
- **Purpose**: Undergraduate admission, primarily US; accepted everywhere SAT is accepted (concordance tables link ACT 1–36 to SAT 400–1600)
- **Format**: "Enhanced ACT" (national rollout Sept 6, 2025; online from April 2025): English, Math, Reading core (131 Qs, 2h05m); **Science now optional** (add-on ~40 min); Writing optional essay. Composite = average of core sections (1–36); STEM score reported only if Science taken. Offered digital AND paper
- **Cost**: ~$68 core (2025–26, varies by state/testing option); Science add-on ~$5; Writing add-on ~$25. International pricing set separately and higher (approximate; verify at act.org)
- **Frequency**: 7 national test dates/year (Sep, Oct, Dec, Feb, Apr, Jun, Jul)
- **Score validity**: No official expiry
- **Popularity**: 1,374,791 test-takers in the class of 2024 (ACT national profile); roughly 40% of US testing market vs SAT
- **Prep-hub content scope**: Core English/Math/Reading plus optional Science content; timing drills for the shortened format; adaptive mock engine; ACT→SAT concordance calculator; note content differs between pre-2025 legacy and Enhanced ACT — hub must target the new format
- **Relevance (international)**: **3** (most international US-bound students choose SAT; ACT is the alternative)

## AP Exams (Advanced Placement)
- **Full name + body**: Advanced Placement Examinations, College Board (US)
- **Purpose**: Undergraduate admissions signal + university credit/placement worldwide (US, Canada, UK entry offers, many European/Asian systems); ~40 subjects
- **Format**: Per-subject exams scored 1–5. Major 2025 shift: most exams now fully digital or hybrid digital (Bluebook), excluding studio/art portfolios. Free-response + multiple choice
- **Cost**: $99 per exam (2025 and 2026 administrations confirmed); $147 for AP Seminar/Research; $40 late/unused/cancel fee; $37 fee reduction for eligible low-income students. Outside the US the base fee is the same but local centres/schools add admin fees (varies widely; China/HK/India administered via authorized centres at higher local prices)
- **Frequency**: Once a year, first two weeks of May (late-testing window late May)
- **Score validity**: No expiry (scores archived but retained; some universities apply their own recency preferences)
- **Popularity**: ~5.7M exams taken by 3M+ students in 2024; nearly 6M projected for 2025 (College Board/Washington Times reporting)
- **Prep-hub content scope**: Subject-by-subject hubs (Calculus, Chemistry, Biology, Physics 1/2/C, CS, Economics, Psychology, etc.); MC + FRQ banks with official released questions; digital exam format practice; 1–5 score calculators; credit-transfer guides per university
- **Relevance (international)**: **4** (strongest subject-exam currency for international applicants to US/Canada; increasingly used for UK/EU conditional offers)

## IB Diploma Programme Exams
- **Full name + body**: International Baccalaureate Diploma Programme examinations, International Baccalaureate Organization (IBO, Geneva)
- **Purpose**: Full secondary qualification used directly for university admission worldwide (UK, EU, Canada, Australia, US credit); 6 subjects (HL/SL) + Theory of Knowledge + Extended Essay + CAS
- **Format**: Written exams (papers 1–3 per subject) in May (main) and November (mostly Asia-Pacific) sessions; internal assessments moderated externally. Scores 1–7 per subject, max 45 points (core TOK/EE adds up to 3)
- **Cost**: IB bills schools, not students: candidate registration ~$170–180 + per-subject fees; typical US all-in $120–130/subject; full Diploma typically ~$700–1,200 out of pocket (varies significantly by school and country — approximate)
- **Frequency**: Two sessions/year (May, November)
- **Score validity**: No expiry
- **Popularity**: 192,866 DP+CP candidates in May 2024 session (up 7.2% YoY); ~202k in May 2025; ~3,300 schools in 150+ countries (IBO)
- **Prep-hub content scope**: Per-subject HL/SL syllabi; past-paper banks (heavily searched commodity); IA/Extended Essay/TOK guidance; grade-boundary tables; university conversion/entry requirements by country
- **Relevance (international)**: **4** (the qualification itself, not just a test — but only for students in IB schools)

## GCE A-Levels and GCSEs
- **Full name + body**: General Certificate of Education Advanced Level; General Certificate of Secondary Education. Boards: AQA, OCR, Edexcel (Pearson), WJEC/Eduqas (UK); Cambridge International (CAIE) and Pearson Edexcel International for overseas candidates
- **Purpose**: UK university entry qualification (offers are conditional on A-level grades); also used internationally (Commonwealth, EU, Asia) for direct university entry; GCSEs are prerequisite credentials screened by admissions
- **Format**: A-levels: subject-based exams at end of 2-year course, graded A*–E; GCSEs graded 9–1 (England). Modular in some international boards (AS/A2 units). Mostly written papers; NEA/coursework in select subjects
- **Cost**: Free at UK state schools; private/international candidates pay per subject via exam centres/British Council — typically ~£100–£500 per A-level subject depending on board, units and country (varies widely, approximate)
- **Frequency**: Main May/June session; October/November session for international boards (CAIE, Edexcel International); GCSE Nov resits (English/Maths, England)
- **Score validity**: No expiry
- **Popularity**: 825,355 A-level entries (England, summer 2024); 5.81M GCSE entries England / ~6.19M UK-wide 2024 (JCQ/Ofqual); Cambridge International handles 1M+ entries/year globally incl. IGCSE
- **Prep-hub content scope**: Per-subject revision hubs tied to exam-board specifications; past papers + mark schemes by board and session; grade-boundary trackers; predicted-grade/Ucas tariff explainers; international vs UK board differentiation
- **Relevance (international)**: **5** for UK-bound applicants; **3–4** globally as a curriculum qualification

## GRE General Test
- **Full name + body**: Graduate Record Examinations General Test, ETS (US)
- **Purpose**: Graduate (master's/PhD) admission, primarily US and Canada; also accepted by many European/Asian business and grad programs. Many programs remain test-optional
- **Format**: Shortened GRE (since Sept 22, 2023): ~1h58m. Verbal Reasoning 27 Qs/41 min; Quantitative Reasoning 27 Qs/47 min; one Analytical Writing task ("Analyze an Issue")/30 min. Section-adaptive. Scores 130–170 per section (260–340) + writing 0–6
- **Cost**: $220 in most countries; ~$233 China; minor variations elsewhere (India ~$220–230). Reschedule $50
- **Frequency**: Year-round at test centers and at home; once every 21 days, max 5x/12 months
- **Score validity**: 5 years
- **Popularity**: Roughly 300–400k worldwide per testing year (approximate; ETS no longer publishes a full annual snapshot — 2023–24 top markets: India ~113k, US ~98k, China ~58k)
- **Prep-hub content scope**: Verbal (text completion, sentence equivalence, reading), quant (arithmetic/algebra/geometry/data analysis), AWA templates; adaptive mock tests; vocab tools; GRE↔GMAT concordance; program test-policy database (valued given test-optional ambiguity)
- **Relevance (international)**: **4** for grad school (many competitive programs still expect it, especially for international applicants)

## GMAT (Focus Edition)
- **Full name + body**: Graduate Management Admission Test (Focus Edition), GMAC (US)
- **Purpose**: MBA and business master's admission worldwide; the de facto standard for top MBA programs
- **Format**: 2h15m, 3 section-adaptive sections: Quantitative Reasoning 21 Qs/45 min; Verbal Reasoning 23 Qs/45 min; Data Insights 20 Qs/45 min (integrated reasoning + data sufficiency merged; on-screen calculator). Per-section review and one answer edit allowed. Score 205–805
- **Cost**: ~$275 test center; ~$300 online (varies slightly by country)
- **Frequency**: Year-round, center and online; max 5x/12 months, 8 lifetime, 16-day gap
- **Score validity**: 5 years
- **Popularity**: 115,286 exams taken in TY2024 (Jul 2023–Jun 2024); 93,196 in TY2025 (GMAC); down from 250k+ peak in 2017
- **Prep-hub content scope**: Quant fundamentals, critical reasoning/RC, Data Insights (the new differentiator); adaptive mocks with 205–805 scoring; error log tool; score-to-school (650/700/755 target) guides; Focus vs legacy 10th-edition concordance content for older benchmarks
- **Relevance (international)**: **4** for MBA/business master's applicants (GRE is the main substitute)

## LSAT
- **Full name + body**: Law School Admission Test, LSAC (US/Canada)
- **Purpose**: JD (law) admission in US and Canada; ABA rules now permit GRE for JD, but LSAT remains dominant; also used by some Australian/Indian law programs
- **Format**: Digital (tablet at centers, or remotely proctored). Since Aug 2024 the Analytical Reasoning ("Logic Games") section was removed; current format: Logical Reasoning ×2 + Reading Comprehension (one 35-min section each) + unscored writing sample. Score 120–180
- **Cost**: $238 (2024–25); $248 (2025–26, per LSAC); CAS subscription ~$207–219 separate
- **Frequency**: ~9–10 administrations/year (Aug, Sep/Oct, Nov, Jan, Feb, Apr, Jun, Jul)
- **Score validity**: LSAC reports scores for 5 years
- **Popularity**: 73,390 distinct test-takers / ~155,070 sittings in TY2023–24; 85,554 distinct takers in TY2024–25 (LSAC); applicant volumes rising sharply in 2025–26 (+~20% YoY)
- **Prep-hub content scope**: Logical reasoning question taxonomy (the core), reading comprehension, writing sample; logic-games material is now obsolete — content must reflect the Aug 2024 change; timed digital practice UI; 120–180 score converter; law-school GPA/LSAT medians database
- **Relevance (international)**: **5** for US/Canada JD applicants

## MCAT
- **Full name + body**: Medical College Admission Test, AAMC (US)
- **Purpose**: MD/DO admission in US and Canada; also required/accepted by some Australian and Caribbean medical schools
- **Format**: Computer-based, ~7h30m with breaks. Chemical & Physical Foundations (59 Qs/95 min); CARS (53 Qs/90 min); Biological & Biochemical Foundations (59 Qs/95 min); Psychological, Social & Biological Foundations (59 Qs/95 min). Score 472–528 (CARS 118–132, others 125–131)
- **Cost**: ~$340–345 standard (2025, approximate; verify at aamc.org); +$120 international fee if testing outside US/Canada; ~$140 via Fee Assistance Program; late/reschedule fees extra
- **Frequency**: ~30 test dates Jan–Sept; limits of 3 attempts/year, 4 in 2 years, 7 lifetime
- **Score validity**: Typically 3 years (varies by school; AAMC releases scores for 3 years)
- **Popularity**: 85,000+ examinees/year (AAMC; ~85–88k range)
- **Prep-hub content scope**: Full science content review (biochem, chem/phys, bio, psych/soc), CARS strategy; spaced-repetition flashcards (Anki ecosystem is central); full-length 7.5h simulated exams; 472–528 score converters; school median-GPA/MCAT databases
- **Relevance (international)**: **5** for North American MD applicants

## UCAT
- **Full name + body**: University Clinical Aptitude Test, UCAT Consortium (UK); separate UCAT ANZ run in Australia/NZ
- **Purpose**: Required by nearly all UK medicine and dentistry programs (adopted by Oxford, Cambridge, Imperial, UCL etc. after BMAT's withdrawal); UCAT ANZ required by most Australian medical schools for some entry routes
- **Format**: Computer-based, ~2h. Subtests: Verbal Reasoning, Decision Making, Quantitative Reasoning, Situational Judgement. Cognitive subtests each scaled 300–900 (total 900–2700); SJT reported in Bands 1–4
- **Cost**: £70 UK; £115 international (tests taken outside the UK); bursaries available
- **Frequency**: One annual window, July–early September (single registration in summer)
- **Score validity**: One admission cycle only (results cannot be carried over)
- **Popularity**: ~35–40k+ candidates/year; record ~40k+ in 2024 (approximate — UCAT publishes annual test statistics)
- **Prep-hub content scope**: Timed VR/DM/QR/SJT question banks with official-style pacing; decision-making diagramming drills; SJT band strategy; UK med-school UCAT cutoffs/deadline database; calculator-free mental math trainer
- **Relevance (international)**: **5** for UK medicine/dentistry applicants

## GAMSAT
- **Full name + body**: Graduate Medical School Admissions Test, ACER (Australia)
- **Purpose**: Graduate-entry medicine (and some dentistry/pharmacy) in Australia, UK, and Ireland; also some direct-entry Australian routes
- **Format**: Long-form reasoning exam (~4.5h+ over a test day, now digital): Section I Reasoning in Humanities & Social Sciences; Section II Written Communication (2 essays); Section III Reasoning in Biological & Physical Sciences (assumes ~first-year uni chem/bio + Year-12 physics). No fixed pass mark; used in combination with GPA/interview
- **Cost**: 2025: ~AU$560 (Australia), ~£292 (UK), ~€372 (Ireland) — increases annually
- **Frequency**: Two sittings/year (March and September). Australia uses both; GAMSAT UK: September; GAMSAT Ireland: March
- **Score validity**: 4 years (Australia) / 2 years (UK & Ireland); some individual universities apply shorter windows
- **Popularity**: Not officially published — low tens of thousands globally per year (approximate)
- **Prep-hub content scope**: Section III science reasoning content (the biggest prep need), humanities reasoning, essay-writing frameworks; full-length mock exams with scaled scoring; ACER-style question banks; per-university GPA/score combination explainers
- **Relevance (international)**: **4** for graduate-entry medicine in AU/UK/IE

## IMAT
- **Full name + body**: International Medical Admissions Test, administered for the Italian Ministry of Education (MIUR/MUR) — the sole entry gate to English-taught Medicine & Surgery at Italian public universities
- **Purpose**: Undergraduate medicine (English track) in Italy; national merit ranking; ~1,049 public seats (2025)
- **Format**: Paper-based, 60 questions/100 min: Logical Reasoning & General Knowledge 20; Biology 18; Chemistry 12; Physics 8; Maths 2. Scoring +1.5 correct, −0.4 wrong (max 90). Single September sitting
- **Cost**: ~€130 (some international centres €130–164, varies)
- **Frequency**: Once a year (mid-September), with August registration on Universitaly
- **Score validity**: Current admission cycle only
- **Popularity**: 10,931 candidates (2024); 13,495 (2025) — rising
- **Prep-hub content scope**: A-level-level Biology/Chemistry content (heavily biology-weighted), logic and general-knowledge drills, negative-marking strategy, past-paper bank (2011–2025), seat/cutoff tables per university, non-EU vs EU quota guidance
- **Relevance (international)**: **5** for its niche — nearly all English-track Italy medicine applicants are international

## CASPer
- **Full name + body**: CASPer (Computer-Based Assessment for Sampling Personal Characteristics), Acuity Insights (Canada) — situational judgment test; often paired with the Duet value-alignment assessment
- **Purpose**: Used by 100+ programs in US/Canada (medicine, nursing, PA), several UK medical schools, and Australian programs (nursing, teaching, medicine)
- **Format**: Online, ~90–110 min. 12–15 scenarios (video + text) with typed and video follow-up responses; rated by raters, reported to programs as relative performance (percentile/quartile)
- **Cost**: ~$85 USD (2025–26 US cycle) including distribution to an included set of programs; ~$18–20 per additional program; Duet ~$40; varies by country/program type
- **Frequency**: Multiple test dates in rolling windows aligned to each country's application season (year-round availability by region)
- **Score validity**: One admission cycle (1 year)
- **Popularity**: 100,000+ applicants/year, including ~80% of US and Canadian medical school applicants (Acuity Insights)
- **Prep-hub content scope**: Scenario banks with model responses, typing-speed/pace training, ethics frameworks (P.I.E. etc.), video-response practice, program requirement database, example rated responses
- **Relevance (international)**: **3** (broad but shallow requirement — a gate for a subset of health programs)

## UK University Admissions Tests (ESAT, TMUA, TSA, MAT, STEP)
- **Full names + bodies**:
  - **ESAT** — Engineering and Science Admissions Test (UAT-UK, delivered via Pearson VUE). Cambridge + Imperial. Replaced **NSAA and ENGAA** from the Oct 2024 cycle (2025 entry)
  - **TMUA** — Test of Mathematics for University Admission (UAT-UK/Pearson VUE). Cambridge (Economics, Computer Science), Warwick, LSE, Durham, Bath and others; Imperial Mathematics switched to TMUA from 2025 entry
  - **TSA** — Thinking Skills Assessment (Oxford; computer-based via Pearson VUE since Oct 2024). PPE, Economics & Management, Experimental Psychology and related courses
  - **MAT** — Mathematics Admissions Test (Oxford; online via Pearson VUE since 2024–25). Oxford Maths/CS; Warwick historically accepted it (largely moved to TMUA/STEP)
  - **STEP** — Sixth Term Examination Paper in Mathematics (administered by OCR on behalf of Cambridge). Conditional-offer requirement for Cambridge Mathematics (and some Warwick/others)
- **Purpose**: Undergraduate admission to Oxford, Cambridge, Imperial and other selective UK STEM/economics courses
- **Format**:
  - ESAT: computer-based, modular — Mathematics 1 plus two of {Maths 2, Physics, Chemistry, Biology}; ~40-min MCQ modules; reported 1.0–9.0
  - TMUA: two 75-min MCQ papers; score 1.0–9.0
  - TSA: 90-min thinking-skills MCQ (+30-min essay for some courses)
  - MAT: computer-based maths problems (no calculator)
  - STEP: handwritten-style maths papers (STEP 2 and STEP 3), graded S/1/2/3, sat in June
- **Cost**: ESAT/TMUA £75 UK & Ireland / £130 international (UAT-UK bursaries available); Oxford's tests (TSA, MAT, BMSAT etc.) are free; STEP ~£102.50 per paper UK / ~£142.25 international (recent cycle, via OCR)
- **Frequency**: ESAT/TMUA: October sitting (required by Cambridge) + January sitting (for other universities); TSA/MAT: once a year, late October/early November; STEP: once a year, June
- **Score validity**: Current admission cycle only (STEP results used in the offer year)
- **Popularity**: Low thousands to ~10k per test annually (approximate — e.g., ESAT/TMUA candidates number in the high single-digit thousands; STEP a few thousand)
- **Prep-hub content scope**: Past-paper banks (MAT/STEP/TSA have deep archives; NSAA/ENGAA→ESAT mapping for legacy practice); A-level-beyond maths/physics content for ESAT/MAT/STEP; timed online mock UIs mirroring Pearson VUE; per-course module requirements table (critical for ESAT); Oxford Jan-2026 announcement: from the 2027 entry cycle Oxford will replace bespoke tests (MAT, PAT, TSA...) with UAT-UK standardized tests — a hub should build for the consolidated UAT-UK ecosystem
- **Relevance (international)**: **5** within their segments (Cambridge/Oxford/Imperial STEM applicants must sit them)

## Discontinued / avoid (do NOT build prep hubs)
- **SAT Subject Tests** — discontinued by College Board in January 2021 (final international sitting June 2021). Redirect traffic to AP content instead
- **SAT Essay** — dropped June 2021
- **Paper SAT** — replaced by Digital SAT (international from March 2023; US from March 2024). Legacy paper content only useful as archive
- **BMAT (BioMedical Admissions Test)** — final administration October 2023; all UK medicine users (Oxford, Cambridge, Imperial, UCL, etc.) moved to UCAT; Oxford Biomedical Sciences now uses its own **BMSAT** (2025 entry). No future demand
- **NSAA and ENGAA** — replaced by ESAT (Oct 2024). Keep only as redirect/legacy content
- **Old GMAT (10th edition, 200–800 scale)** — retired Feb 1, 2024; only the Focus Edition (205–805) is administered
- **Old long GRE (pre-Sept 2023)** — replaced by the shortened format; mark legacy content clearly
- **Pre-2025 classic ACT (always-on Science)** — replaced nationally by the Enhanced ACT (Sept 2025); Science optional. Prep content must not teach the old mandatory-Science composite
- **UKCAT** — renamed UCAT in 2019 (search traffic still uses "UKCAT"; capture as keyword, not a separate hub)
- **Oxford's cancelled written tests** — GAT, ELAT, AMELAT cancelled from 2025 entry; MAT/PAT/TSA slated for replacement by UAT-UK tests from the 2027 entry cycle (announced Jan 2026)
